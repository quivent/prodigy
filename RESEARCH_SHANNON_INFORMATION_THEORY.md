## Phase 2: Investigation

### Claude Elwood Shannon (1916-2001)

**Background**:
- American mathematician, electrical engineer, and cryptographer
- Often called "the father of information theory"
- Worked at Bell Labs, later MIT professor
- Created mathematical theory of communication in 1948

**Key Contributions**:
- Information Theory foundational paper: "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" (1948)
- Digital circuit design theory
- Cryptography and coding theory
- Applied Boolean algebra to electronic circuits

### Information Theory Core Principles

#### 1. Information as Measurable Quantity
**Fundamental Insight**: Information can be quantified mathematically
- Information content is inversely related to probability
- More surprising events carry more information
- Common events carry little information

#### 2. Entropy (H)
**Definition**: Measure of uncertainty or randomness in information
**Formula**: H(X) = -Σ p(x) log₂ p(x)
**Key Insights**:
- Higher entropy = more uncertainty/information
- Maximum entropy when all outcomes equally probable
- Zero entropy when outcome is certain

#### 3. Channel Capacity
**Concept**: Maximum rate at which information can be transmitted reliably
**Shannon's Channel Capacity Theorem**: C = B log₂(1 + S/N)
- C = channel capacity (bits per second)
- B = bandwidth (Hz)
- S/N = signal-to-noise ratio

#### 4. Source Coding (Compression)
**Shannon's Source Coding Theorem**: 
- Information can be compressed to its entropy limit
- No lossless compression can go below entropy
- Optimal codes assign shorter symbols to more frequent events

#### 5. Channel Coding (Error Correction)
**Shannon's Noisy Channel Theorem**:
- Reliable communication possible even with noise
- Error rates can be made arbitrarily small
- Requires redundancy and clever encoding
