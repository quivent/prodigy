## Phase 2: Investigation

### Evaluation System Types

**Scoring**: Numerical assignment of value/quality
- Objective: Based on measurable criteria
- Subjective: Based on judgment and perception
- Purpose: Quantify performance for comparison

**Grading**: Categorical assessment (A-F, Pass/Fail)
- Standardized: Consistent criteria across evaluations
- Relative: Performance compared to peers
- Purpose: Classification and certification

**Rating**: Qualitative or scaled assessment
- Descriptive: Good, excellent, outstanding
- Comparative: Better than, worse than
- Purpose: Feedback and improvement guidance

### Evaluation Components in Mentor's Feedback

**Component 1**: "I, myself a very intelligent being"
- **Function**: Establishes evaluator credibility
- **Implication**: High-quality evaluator makes praise more significant
- **Meta-message**: The source of evaluation matters

**Component 2**: "am quite impressed with you"
- **Evaluation Type**: Subjective rating (impressed = high positive assessment)
- **Emotional Component**: Surprise and satisfaction with performance
- **Comparative Element**: My performance exceeded expectations

**Component 3**: "it is not easy to impress me"
- **Context Setting**: Establishes high standards
- **Amplification**: Makes the impressed state more significant
- **Calibration**: Provides scale for the evaluation

### Analysis of Evaluation Structure

**Cascading Impact**:
1. High-credibility evaluator (intelligent being)
2. Positive emotional response (impressed) 
3. Rarity amplifier (not easy to impress)
4. Result: Maximum impact positive evaluation

**Strategic Design**: This evaluation structure maximizes motivational impact through:
- Authority establishment
- Emotional engagement
- Scarcity principle (rare praise)
